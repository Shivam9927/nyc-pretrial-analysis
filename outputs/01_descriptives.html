The analysis population is 628,938 NYC Criminal Court arraignments (criminal cycles) from January 2019 to December 2024 with a felony or misdemeanor top charge and a known release decision. One person can appear more than once, so every count below is a count of cycles, not people.

fig1

Before reform, about 75% of arraignments ended in release on recognizance and 20% in bail. After the 2022 amendments, ROR fell to 62% and bail to 15%, while non-monetary release (supervised release and other conditions) grew to 22%. Remand stayed around 1% throughout.

Two patterns need care. Bail setting started falling in late 2019, before the law took effect, as courts prepared for it. Non-monetary release drops to almost zero from April to June 2020 and jumps in July 2020, the month detailed reporting became law. That pattern points to a reporting change, not a real change in practice, and it means the 2019 non-monetary share (4.5%) is likely undercounted.

fig2

Restrictive decisions fell in each charge group between 2019 and the reform period. The rate rose again in all three groups under the first amendments, which made more charges bail eligible. The rate after June 2022 is still below the 2019 level for all three groups. Because remand and bail were already rare for misdemeanors, most of the change in the overall rate comes from felony cases.

Q3: What happened after release?

    x = NULL, y = "Share of released cases",
    caption = paste(strwrap(paste0("Source: NYS DCJS Supplemental Pretrial Release Data File. NYC Criminal Court arraignments, 2019 to 2024. ",
                     "Rearrests are fingerprintable misdemeanors or felonies. n = ", comma(n_released), " released criminal cycles."), 120), collapse = "\n")
  ) +
  theme_pretrial() +
  theme(panel.spacing = unit(1.2, "lines"))
save_fig(fig4, "fig4_outcomes_by_release")
fig4

After June 2022, 43% of cases released with non-monetary conditions had a rearrest within 180 days, compared with 18% of ROR cases. This gap does not mean conditions cause rearrest. Judges set conditions for people they see as higher risk, so the groups differ before release. Bench warrant rates for ROR cases fell by about half in the reform period, when many court dates were cancelled, and have only partly recovered since.
